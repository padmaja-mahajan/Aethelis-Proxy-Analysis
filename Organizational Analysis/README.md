# Aethelis Proxy Organizational Analysis

## Introduction
Name of the organization: Aethelis Proxy

1. Why was the analysis done

   This analysis was conducted to understand the structure of Aethelis' workforce and organization in a way that supports practical workforce planning and leadership decision-making. The report examines how employees are distributed across business units, departments, subdepartments, and teams; how management layers are positioned; and where the workforce is located across regions and cities. The aim is to identify organizational patterns, workforce concentration, and management capacity issues that can inform hiring, restructuring, and planning activities.

2. The findings from the analysis

   - The modeled workforce includes **3,124 employees**, of which **2,474 are active** and **650 are former employees**.
   - The organization is structured across **6 business units**, **26 departments**, and **135 subdepartments**.

   - Active Headcount - For 2474 active employees and 227 active teams,
     - *Team Size Distribution & Manager Span of Control*
       - The team structure is largely consistent: 39% of teams have 11-20 members, and 74% of managers lead 7-15 direct reports.
       - Product & Technology accounts for a third of the workforce.
       - Span of Control: About 11% of managers have 6 or fewer reports, which points to possible layering. About 15% have more than 15, which is a capacity and quality-of-management risk.
       - Outliers: Team sizes range from 1 to 36. The smallest and largest teams are the clearest candidates for restructuring.
      
     - *Grades*
       - G1-G3 hold 1,198 people (48.4%), and G5-G8 hold 1,202 (48.6%). The workforce is split almost evenly between junior and senior grades.
       - G5 is almost 8x larger than G4. G4 is the *bottleneck*.
       - G7-G8 hold 266 people (10.75%). This seems a modest top.
       - It's an hourglass structure and not a classic pyramid.

     - *Geographical Presence*
       - The workforce is heavily concentrated in India, with Bengaluru as the primary hub. Presence outside India is small and limited to regional offices.  
       - **Footprint:** Active employees are spread across 10 locations in 5 countries and 3 regions (APAC, EMEA and the Americas).
       - **Concentration in India:** India holds 2,409 of 2,474 employees (97%), spread across six cities.
       - **Bengaluru is the anchor:** The Headquarters has 1,620 employees, 65% of the active workforce. The next largest sites are Hyderabad (208, 8%) and Pune (170, 7%), both Development Centers.
       - **Small Regional offices:** Mumbai (168) and Chennai (126) are significant, but the sites in Singapore (20), New York (20), London (18) and Sydney (7) together hold about 3% of the workforce.
       - **Location mix:** One Headquarters, three Development Centers, and regional offices or a hub elsewhere. Most delivery capacity sits in India.
   
   - The report highlights operational patterns that matter for leadership planning, including team size distribution, department composition, and geographic workforce concentration.

## Organization overview

Aethelis Proxy is a global, mature, matrixed FinTech SaaS organization founded in 2011 and headquartered in Bengaluru, India. It operates in the B2B financial technology sector and serves global customers. Its workforce and organizational structure in this project are synthetic and are designed for workforce and people analytics.

Aethelis Proxy's geographic footprint is centered in India, with international offices in Singapore, the United Kingdom, the United States, and Australia. The Power BI report includes a city-level employee presence view to examine how the workforce is distributed across locations.

## Recommendations

*Team Size Distribution & Manager Span of Control*

- Look at managers with more than 15 reports for support or a restructure.
- Look at managers with 3 or fewer reports for possible delayering.

*Grades*

- The grade structure is hourglass. G4 is unusually small, which may indicate a promotion bottleneck or grade-mapping issue. G5 could be the standard for lateral grades. Check G5 by BU and department, to see whether the bulge is concentrated.
- Tenure by grade. If G5 has short tenure, they were hired in. If it has long tenure, they’re stuck.
- Are employees at G4 leaving?

*Geographical Presence*

- Site concentration risk: 66.5% of the workforce sits at Bengaluru. This affects talent competition in one labor market, and exposure to local disruption. How is the workmode of employees? The Workmode Policy, future projects, retention plans and hiring requirements may need to be looked at if required.
- Thin presence outside India: The offices in the UK, US, Singapore and Australia are small (7-20 people each), which may limit client coverage, time-zone overlap and local leadership depth.
- Talent pipeline: Hyderabad and Pune are the natural growth centers if the goal is to reduce dependence on Bengaluru.
